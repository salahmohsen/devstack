What works

Maximum fidelity to what the PDF will be — resume and cover letter read as real documents, exactly like onboarding. Nothing competes with the paper; hover cards keep originals available without clutter.

What risks

Include switches and evidence are one hover deep — reviewing many items means lots of hovering. Email controls (To, squares, Send) must fit under the mail without crowding it.

Pick this if

You want the onboarding feel most literally: the document is the product, controls are quiet, and trust comes from seeing exactly what will render.

Resume in D — full paper from the attachment card

Same paper as A/B/C — reached via sidebar or the attachment card's jump.

What works

The send is the story: to, mail, attachments, and Send resolve in one glance. Attachment cards double as navigation — "Edit on paper" jumps into the full document experience.

What risks

Tailoring quality is the other half of the value; burying resume/cover behind a click undersells the edit work. Two-column stage is tightest for the A4 paper.

Pick this if

Most sessions end in Send — you want the mail and attachments to be the first thing you see, with documents a deliberate click away.

How to pick a direction

All four honor the grill locks: near-fullscreen dialog, sidebar sections, live papers, evidence hover, generic Matte mail, Save/Send. They differ in where controls live.

Paper Studio (A)If the document should be the whole show and controls stay quiet — most onboarding-like.
Workbench (B)If you review often and want include switches, originals, and mail tools permanently visible.
Guided (C)If you want onboarding's step-and-tick feel with an item rail driving the paper.
Composer-first (D)If Send is the destination and mail + attachments should land first.
