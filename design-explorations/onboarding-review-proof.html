The review proof surface — four directions

The Review step must prove one thing: what you confirm is what the resume says. Today the doc pane is a squeezed wall of text while the confirm pane floats in dead space. All four directions below are drawn with your real draft record — the dense one, with empty education and certifications — so the placeholders carry real weight.

What works

One reading order — you verify each row against its own doc excerpt without eye travel. Chapters make empty sections feel designed, and the sticky footer never floats in dead space because there is no second column to outgrow.

Risk

You lose the "whole resume at once" proof — the artifact is always partial on screen. Users who want to see the final document whole must trust the excerpt or wait for the PDF.

Pick when

Simplicity and mobile parity matter most, and you accept the doc as a per-section mirror rather than a single artifact.

C

Balanced proof

The current split, repaired: 50/50 panes, doc with paper measure and breathing room, confirm rows as full-width list rows (no multi-column tiles), footer pinned to the pane. Lowest rework; still two competing reading areas.

What works

Everything that exists stays — tabs, counts, portfolio switches — but the confirm side stops wasting space: one row per experience, full width, with resume presence stated in words ("3 highlights on the resume"). The footer pins to the pane so it never floats mid-screen.

Risk

Two side-by-side reading areas remain — the eye still shuttles. At below-lg the doc still collapses to a strip, so the "proof" is weaker on small screens than A or B.

Pick when

You want the biggest visible improvement for the least structural change, and heavy inline editing must keep its current home.

D

Focus sections

A compact doc strip (mini paper + section ticks) stays pinned on top; below it, one section workspace at a time at full page width with a section stepper. Maximum editing comfort; the live doc becomes a peek, not a companion.

What works

Full-width rows with big targets — the most comfortable editing surface of the four, and the section stepper doubles as a progress map. The mini paper keeps the metaphor alive without stealing height.

Risk

You never see the whole document while editing — the strip is a summary. The metaphor degrades from "living document" to "fancy checklist with a preview".

Pick when

Editing comfort wins: dense drafts (19 skills, long bullets) where inline field editing is the real work.

Which direction?

All four keep: section ticks (never %), "not on this resume" placeholders, instant checkbox→doc reflection, and the sticky apply footer with counts + source.

A — Paper first if the resume should be the star and editing can live in a focused rail/panel.

B — One scroll if you want the simplest honest flow with per-section verification and no split at all.

C — Balanced proof if you want the current structure, fixed: real proportions, list rows, pinned footer.

D — Focus sections if inline editing comfort matters more than the always-visible document.
