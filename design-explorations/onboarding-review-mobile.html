What works

Touch users keep the proven rich-text editor for long text — no thumb-typing into a narrow paper. The strip still proves section presence; the paper peek keeps the metaphor without pretending it's editable.

Risk

Row editors differ by breakpoint (description field only below lg) — one conditional, but the two experiences must be tested as distinct surfaces.

Pick when

You want mobile to stay simple and honest, and you accept paper editing as a desktop affordance.

One editing model on every screen size — learn it once. The pencils make the affordance touch-honest (no hover needed), and long text gets the paper's full width rather than a form field.

Risk

Editing dense resume text in a ~440px textarea is thumb-hostile; IME keyboards cover the paper. Rich formatting is unavailable on touch with no fallback surface.

Pick when

Consistency outranks comfort, and most mobile sessions are reviewing/unticking rather than rewriting.

What works

Truest to the desktop metaphor: the paper is the first thing you see, whole and tappable, and the footer stays put. No separate strip state to design.

Risk

The paper eats the viewport before any row is reachable — 260px+ of scroll before the first checkbox. Tap-to-edit in a scrolled paper has the same thumb problems as M2.

Pick when

The proof moment matters more on mobile than edit speed, and sessions are mostly verification.

Which small-screen model?

All three share: section ticks, confirmed-only content, sticky footer with counts + source, and the same draft state as desktop.

M1 — Strip + row editors if mobile keeps the full form editor and the paper stays a read-only proof.

M2 — Tap-to-edit paper if one editing model should rule every screen size.

M3 — Full-width paper + accordion if the mobile moment should be verification-first, paper-forward.
