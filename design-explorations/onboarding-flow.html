What works

Ships as incremental polish — chips summarizing counts, sticky selection footer, per-row inline edit. Zero new mental model; every existing test and interaction survives.

What risks

Doesn't change the fundamental cost of Review: long resumes still mean many rows in one scroll, and jobs/connect stay two separate stops after it.

Pick this if

You want the highest ratio of UX gain to build cost this quarter, and Review pain is annoyance — not churn.

B

Review cockpit — entity rail + inspector

Optimizes for control on the heaviest step: a persistent entity rail (counts, select-all, jump) beside a scrollable inspector, and jobs/connect become inline continuations of Review instead of separate pages. Tradeoff biggest build of the four; light resumes get a cockpit that's heavier than their cargo.

What works

The rail makes draft size legible at a glance (counts, done states); the inspector kills the expand-edit-collapse dance; collapsing jobs/connect into "Next up" removes two page loads.

What risks

Heaviest build: two-pane responsive behavior (mobile falls back to A's shape), and a cockpit layout overkill for a 2-experience resume needs a graceful empty-cargo mode.

Pick this if

Your target users import dense senior CVs and churn at Review today — control and density are worth the build.

C

Document-first — watch your resume build itself

Optimizes for motivation: a live resume document (the actual artifact they came for) fills in with green checkmarks as the draft is confirmed, while jobs/connect compress into one "wire up" stop. Tradeoff the preview must stay honest to the real RenderCV output, and merging two steps risks users skimming filters.

What works

The artifact is the reward: every checkbox visibly "writes" their resume, which is exactly the motivation gap between Review-today and finishing. Completion % gives an honest progress signal.

What risks

The preview must track the real RenderCV renderer or it becomes a lie users remember. Education section shown here is aspirational until the education/certifications plan ships. Merged "Wire up" step can bury job-filter nuance.

Pick this if

Drop-off happens between Analyze and Done — you need the flow itself to sell completion, and you can invest in preview fidelity.

D

Zero-friction — analyze on upload, one required stop

Optimizes for time-to-value: upload fires analysis immediately, Review is the only stop that blocks, and job filters + account wiring become an optional checklist on the Done screen. Tradeoff least guidance — users who need the filter step explained may skip it and get noisy New jobs.

What works

Cuts the wizard from 5 stops to 2. The analyze-on-upload behavior already exists (detached job survives leaving the page), so this mostly re-sequences UI, not infra.

What risks

Job filters silently drive New jobs quality — making them optional-on-done risks empty-signal inboxes that look broken. Review-as-only-stop inherits all of Review's density with no jobs/connect breathing room.

Pick this if

Analytics show most drop-off before Review finishes, and you'd rather ship users to a working dashboard than a finished wizard.

How to pick a direction

A one-line rubric for choosing one of these to take forward.

If you want →cheapest meaningful upgrade to Review, zero flow change — pick A.
If you want →the heaviest step to finally feel controllable and fewer page loads — pick B.
If you want →completion rate moved by motivation, and can invest in preview fidelity — pick C.
If you want →fewest stops and a working dashboard fast, accepting quieter filters — pick D.
