Locked · Q1 — what edits on the paper

Name, headline, summary, titles, orgs, bullet lines. Dates, skills, cert fields, contacts, toggles stay off-paper — no ambiguous inverse mapping.

Locked · Q2 — engagement model

Click-to-edit per field. Real input/textarea, identical typography, commit on blur or ⌘Enter, Esc cancels. The paper always renders the true RenderCV mapping at rest.

Locked · Q3 — deselection

Confirmed rows only. Uncheck → row vanishes from the paper instantly, no toast; an open editor closes; committed edits survive in the draft.

The paper affordance (locked, shown once so we agree on the feel)

Static cue at rest is a text cursor + the pane hint; hover and editing states use the brand green — the paper never changes shape or size when a field opens.

What works

Each surface does the job it's shaped for: quick fields stay inline and instant; the skills drawer gets real room for 19 rows without stretching the rail or veiling the paper for small edits.

Risk

Two editing models to learn (inline expansion vs drawer), and the drawer still veils the paper for skills work. Slightly more code than A1.

Pick when

You want A1's always-visible paper but accept one drawer for the one surface that genuinely needs width — bulk skill management.

Which rail model?

All three share the locked surface: paper-first hero, click-to-edit (green affordance), confirmed-only paper, rail sections with ticks + counts.

A1 — Inline rail if the paper must never be covered and a little in-panel scrolling is fine.

A2 — Drawer if you expect heavy non-paper editing and want the roomiest fields with a rich-text fallback.

A3 — Hybrid if skills batch-editing is the only thing you'd ever want a drawer for.
